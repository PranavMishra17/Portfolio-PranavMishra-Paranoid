What I'm doing now

Alfred_ is an assistant that runs your email, your calendar and the small obligations you keep dropping. It answers over text message, on the web, and out loud. About five thousand people lean on it every day, which means anything I get wrong, somebody feels that morning. I've been the founding engineer on it since April.

I made it stop making things up.

I rebuilt the assistant's working memory so that stating something nobody told it is not a thing it can do. It isn't a paragraph in a prompt asking it politely — the tests fail if it drifts.

Around that sits a risk-scored gate, an eval harness I wrote from scratch, and a scanner that reads real conversations looking for the failure before a person has to report it.

I took the model out of the rules engine.

Email rules are the stickiest thing in the product and used to be the most expensive: a model call per message. I replaced it with a three-stage matcher that reaches the same decision deterministically.

About 30% cheaper per person, and roughly 98% of rules now get made just by talking to it.

I made it fast enough to trust.

An email used to take about ninety seconds to reach you as a text message. Now it takes three. Login codes used to arrive after three minutes at the tail of the distribution; now they're there before you've switched apps.

I gave it five ways to answer, and one of them is no.

Every action the assistant wants to take goes through one router: stay silent, notify, confirm, ask, or refuse. The scoring is deterministic — not the model grading its own homework.

Anything it can't settle waits in a queue you can actually see, and there's a window afterwards where you can take it back.

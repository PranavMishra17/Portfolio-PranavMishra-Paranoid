What I own there

Making it stop inventing things

I rebuilt the assistant's working memory so that fabrication isn't discouraged, it's structurally impossible: the thing can only speak from what it actually holds. Tests hold that line, not good intentions. Around it sits risk-scored gating, an eval harness I wrote from scratch, and a scanner that reads live conversations looking for the failures nobody reported.

The email rules

Rules are the feature people get attached to. Every message used to cost a model call to check. I replaced that with a deterministic three-stage matcher that answers without asking a model: about thirty percent cheaper per user, and now roughly ninety-eight percent of rules get made just by talking to it.

Ninety seconds down to three

An email used to take about ninety seconds to reach you as a text. It takes about three now. The one-time-code path was 189 seconds at the ninetieth percentile. It is instant.

The execution decision layer

Before the agent does anything on your behalf, the action passes a router with five verdicts: stay silent, notify, confirm, clarify, refuse. The risk score is arithmetic, so the model never grades its own homework. What it can't settle waits in a queue, and there is a window in which anything can be undone.

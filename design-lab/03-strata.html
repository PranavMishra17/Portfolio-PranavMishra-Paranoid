Alfred_

Founding LLM Engineer. New York City.

Alfred_ runs people's email, calendar and daily obligations for them, over text message, web chat and voice. More than five thousand people rely on it.

I made it stop making things up. I rebuilt the assistant's working memory so that inventing a fact isn't something it can do — the shape of the data won't allow it, and the tests hold that line instead of me hoping the model behaves.

Every action it wants to take now goes through a decision layer with five possible verdicts: stay silent, notify me, ask me to confirm, ask me a question, or refuse. The risk score behind that is deterministic. The model does not grade its own homework. Anything still waiting sits in a queue, and there's a window in which anything done can be undone.

The email rules are the reason people stay. That used to mean a model call for every message arriving. I replaced it with a matcher that runs in three stages and mostly never needs the model at all. It costs about thirty per cent less per person, and about ninety-eight per cent of rules now get made just by talking to it in plain sentences.

An email used to take about ninety seconds to reach you as a text. Now it takes about three. One-time security codes used to take a hundred and eighty-nine seconds at the slow end. Now they arrive as they land.

I also wrote the evaluation harness from nothing, and a scanner that reads real conversations looking for the failures nobody has reported yet.

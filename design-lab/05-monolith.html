Where I work

Alfred_ is an assistant you talk to by text message, by voice, or in a chat window. It reads your email, keeps your calendar, and carries the small obligations you would otherwise drop. About five thousand people rely on it right now. I was the first engineer, and I took the half that has to be right.

I made it stop making things up

A language model will fill a gap with something plausible. That is charming in a demo and unforgivable when the gap is your calendar. I rebuilt the working memory so the assistant cannot invent a fact about your life. Not discouraged from it. Unable to. Tests hold that line, so it doesn't depend on the model behaving.

Around it sits the rest of the reliability layer: risk scoring on every action, an eval harness I built from nothing, and a scanner that reads live conversations to find the failures nobody bothered to report.

The rules engine

It's the reason people stay. It used to ask a language model about every message that arrived. I replaced that with a matcher in three stages that decides for itself, which costs about thirty percent less per person. Almost all rules, around ninety-eight percent, now get made by someone simply saying what they want.

Waiting

An email took about ninety seconds to reach you as a text. Now it takes about three. Security codes were the worst of it, a hundred and eighty-nine seconds at the ninetieth percentile, and now they land while you're still holding the phone.

Permission

Every action the assistant wants to take goes through one router with five answers: stay silent, notify, confirm, ask, refuse. The scoring is deterministic. I don't let the model be its own judge. What's waiting sits in a queue you can see, and there is a window in which anything done can be undone.

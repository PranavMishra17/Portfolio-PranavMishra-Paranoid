Field Notes

— in order, as I found it
2023 Software Developer Intern Bipolar Factory, Bengaluru

First fieldwork. I was dropped into a live codebase and told to make myself useful, so I did — mostly by asking too many questions and breaking things gently enough to learn how they were built.

Feb 2024 — present Research Software Engineer V-ARE Labs, University of Illinois Chicago

This is where I learned to build things that have to survive contact with real people. I built virtual patient systems in Unreal Engine 5 for bias research in medicine, and an audio ML pipeline that classifies at 98.52% accuracy. Some of it is still running. Some of it taught me what breaks.

Jul 2025 — Mar 2026 AI Engineer WheelPrice, Charlotte NC (remote)

Different terrain — commercial, deadline-driven, less forgiving of a wrong turn. I kept my footing and moved on with it.

Apr 2026 — present Founding LLM Engineer Alfred_, New York City

Alfred_ is a consumer AI executive assistant — a multi-agent system that manages people's email, calendar, and daily obligations over SMS, web chat, and voice. Over 5,000 people trust it with that right now. Part of my job is making sure it keeps deserving it.

  • The reliability layer. Risk-scored gating, an eval harness built from scratch, a scanner that reads live conversations for production failures, and a rebuilt working memory that makes the model fabricating things structurally impossible — enforced by tests, not by hoping it behaves.
  • The email-rules engine — the feature people stick around for. I replaced per-message model calls with a deterministic three-stage matcher: about 30% cheaper per user to run, and roughly 98% of rules now get created just by talking to it.
  • Latency. Email to SMS notification used to take about 90 seconds. Now it's about 3. The security and one-time-code path went from 189 seconds at the 90th percentile to instant.
  • The Execution Decision Layer. Every action the agent wants to take passes through a router with five possible verdicts — stay silent, notify, ask to confirm, ask to clarify, or refuse — decided by a deterministic risk score rather than the model grading its own homework, backed by a pending-obligations queue and a bounded window to undo.

# Candidate concepts — round two

Each is a real-world place or object. Each houses all six data models. Read BRIEF2.md first.

Legend for the mapping table: **A**bout · **E**xperience · **P**rojects · **R**esearch ·
**I**nterests · **S**urprises.

---

## 16 — The Room
*His own idea, and the best one on the list.* A cutaway of a room, drawn flat and clean —
one wall and the floor, in the manner of an architectural section rather than a video-game
render. You look at the whole room at once. Objects are labelled on hover and open on click.

- **A** — a note left on the desk, and the room itself doing the describing.
- **E** — the wardrobe. Four sets of clothes on hangers, one per job. A hi-vis vest, a
  lanyard, a lab coat. Pull the door, see the four.
- **P** — the monitor on the desk is showing something he made; a shelf of game cases beside it.
- **R** — three papers stacked on the desk corner, slightly askew, a pen on top.
- **I** — the bookshelf, and the posters above the bed.
- **S** — the trophy shelf (MIT XR, HINT 5.0), and one small thing that reacts when clicked.

Navigation: the room is the map. No menu. Hover highlights, click opens a panel that sits
*in* the room rather than covering it.

Risk: a room drawn badly looks like clip art. It must be one coherent line weight, one
limited palette, and drawn as if by an architect.

## 17 — The Window
*Fuses the thing he liked (travelling light) with the thing he needs (structure).* You are
sitting at a desk. The window fills most of the frame and shows the landscape from Vantage,
where the light travels from afternoon to night. The desk and the wall around the window
hold everything else. Text sits on solid paper, never on the sky — which fixes the
readability complaint directly.

- **A** — a handwritten card propped against the window frame.
- **E** — four ID badges on lanyards hanging from a hook beside the window.
- **P** — the open laptop on the desk, screen showing the project; arrow keys or a click
  cycles which one is on screen.
- **R** — three offprints in a wire tray.
- **I** — a stack of books at the desk's edge, spines readable; a film ticket used as a bookmark.
- **S** — the sun goes down as you move through the content, and after dark the desk lamp
  comes on and the window becomes a reflection of the room.

Navigation: everything is on one surface. Click an object. The view never leaves the desk.

## 18 — The Card Catalogue
A library card catalogue, face on. A grid of small wooden drawers with typed labels. Pull a
drawer and the cards inside come forward, oversized and readable.

- **A** — the top-left drawer, one card, typed.
- **E** — a drawer of employment cards, each with dates stamped.
- **P** — a drawer where each card has a real screenshot pasted onto it.
- **R** — a drawer of paper cards, catalogued properly, with the accepted one stamped.
- **I** — a drawer of book cards with a checkout slip and due-date stamps in the corner.
- **S** — one unlabelled drawer near the bottom that contains the trophies and whatever
  he's playing.

Navigation: drawers. Nobody needs to be taught a drawer. Only one open at a time, which
enforces the simplicity.

Risk: skeuomorphic wood grain would be hideous. Draw it flat and typographic — the
*grammar* of a catalogue, not a photograph of one.

## 19 — The Facade
A building at night, drawn as a flat elevation. Some windows are lit. Each lit window is a
room, and each room is a data model. Click a window and the view pushes into it; the
building dims and that one room fills the frame.

- **A** — the doorway and the name on the buzzer panel.
- **E** — four windows on one floor, each a former office, lights on in sequence.
- **P** — the workshop on the ground floor, lit brightest, with the screenshots visible inside.
- **R** — a small library window, three volumes on the sill.
- **I** — the flat with the television on and a bookcase behind it.
- **S** — the attic light, on for no stated reason. Click it to find out.

Navigation: lit windows are obviously clickable. Dark ones are obviously not.

## 20 — The Shelf
The most minimal thing on the list. One long horizontal shelf across the middle of the
screen, and a row of objects standing on it. You travel left to right along the shelf.
Nothing else on screen. Each object is a thing, drawn simply and precisely.

- **A** — a framed photograph at the far left, where a shelf always starts.
- **E** — four objects from four workplaces: a transit card, a hard hat, a lab badge.
- **P** — game cases and a small monitor, standing among them.
- **R** — three slim journals, spines out.
- **I** — books, leaning. A DVD case. A controller.
- **S** — the trophies, and one object that is not explained until you pick it up.

Navigation: travel along the line, pick things up. The shelf is the timeline and the menu
at once.

---

## 21 — The Case
A hard travel case, opened flat, foam cut to fit each object it carries. What he takes with
him. Every compartment is shaped like the thing it holds, so the layout itself tells you
what is missing and what is there.

- **A** — the luggage tag.
- **E** — four passport stamps in the lid pocket.
- **P** — the tools in the main foam, each cut to shape.
- **R** — a flat document sleeve.
- **I** — a paperback wedged in the side, a pair of headphones.
- **S** — a small compartment under the foam.

Risk: closest to a "grid of cards" of anything here. It only works if the foam cutouts are
genuinely different shapes.

## Concepts considered and rejected

- **A tree with branches** — he named it explicitly as an example not to build.
- **A star chart / constellation** — already built as variant 15, and it failed.
- **A dossier with manila folder tabs** — his current live site already uses a manila
  folder tab for the experience section; it would read as a retread.
- **A map of a fictional territory** — fails the "no puzzle" rule. Maps need legends.
- **A terminal or a filesystem** — the most obvious developer-portfolio cliché there is.
- **An arcade cabinet** — he is removing a mini-game from the current site, not adding one.

Twelve directions

Throwaway prototypes, built to be judged and thrown away. Each one is a different answer to “a landscape, away from the noise of the internet.”

Open a few. Note which ones you want to keep looking at — that reaction is the whole point of this exercise.

  1. 01VantageOne horizon. Its light travels sunrise to dusk as you move through my life.
  2. 02OrbitYou’re a satellite. Arriving somewhere feels like a docking clamp closing.
  3. 03StrataAlien geology. The work surfaces from between rock layers, miles deep.
  4. 04ThermalThe world in a spectrum eyes don’t have. Current work is the hottest thing in frame.
  5. 05MonolithThe type is the terrain. You walk past words like standing stones.
  6. 06TideThe horizontal one. One unbroken painted dusk you traverse sideways.
  7. 07MaAn ink horizon in a vast pale field. The most austere. Should feel like relief.
  8. 08AuroraTrue black, one ribbon of light. Everything else small and quiet in the dark.
  9. 09Field LogAn expedition log from somewhere that isn’t Earth. Wet, cold, bioluminescent.
  10. 10PauseThe pause screen of a game you’re already inside. The world waits behind it.
  11. 11SignalSomething transmitting from very far away. Content arrives rather than loads.
  12. 12WeatherOne vast sky, and a life kept as a meteorological record beneath it.

Second pass

Built after your feedback. These show the work instead of describing it — real screenshots, and a different treatment for each kind of thing.

  1. 13Double FeatureA repertory cinema. Alfred_ is the marquee, projects are prints you flick through, papers are a programme.
  2. 14Decision LayerYour five-verdict router, playable. Feed it an action, watch it decide. The work proves itself.
  3. 15Deep FieldZoom, don't scroll. A chart on paper becomes a contact sheet becomes a photograph in a darkroom.

Third pass — places and objects

Each one is a real thing, and each of your six data models — about, work, projects, papers, interests, and the odds and ends — has a home inside it. Not sections on a page.

  1. 16The RoomYour idea. A measured elevation of a room, annotated like an architect's drawing.
  2. 17The WindowA desk in front of a window. The light travels as you pick things up; the lamp comes on so reading gets easier, not harder.
  3. 18The CatalogueTwenty labelled drawers. Pull one, a card comes out. The only one where empty interests read as honestly empty.
  4. 19Wall TextMuseum wall labels — the best object ever made for putting the right amount of text beside an image.
  5. 20The CrateA record bin you flip through. Sleeve fronts are the work; the backs are liner notes.
  6. 21The MoveBoxes marked by the city you packed them in. The only layout that encodes your actual biography.
  7. 22The Fridge DoorMagnets, photos, a shopping list. The most personal surface in anyone's home.
  8. 23DeparturesA split-flap board. Built for facts with dates and statuses in them, which is what a career is.
  9. 24Contact Sheet35mm frames with grease-pencil marks. Its whole purpose is showing many images and marking which matter.
  10. 25The PassportStamps in date order. Bengaluru, Chicago, remote, New York — a document for recording where someone has been.

Fourth pass — ten auteurs

No concept from me. Each agent got a sensibility, the material, and hard floors only — then designed the whole thing itself.

  1. 26LynchRoom Tone. One bulb on a cord; everything wired into it. The timecode runs backwards.
  2. 27KubrickFive Parts, Symmetrical. A one-point corridor; every asymmetry is a warm typewritten chip off the axis.
  3. 28KojimaPersonal Archive. A rain-lit strand of eight nodes; the index is the cable, not a nav.
  4. 29RamsYour minimal modernist ask. Screenshots sit greyscale and take colour back only on hover.
  5. 30VignelliThe Unigrid. Your numbers drawn to scale rather than written.
  6. 31Wes AndersonA Catalogue in Nine Drawers. The Department of Personal Taste is filed, labelled and empty.
  7. 32Saul BassA Reliability Picture. A black bar cut three times down to a stub: ninety seconds becomes three.
  8. 33Ikedadata. Opens on 189 counting to zero, before a word of prose. Interests read NO DATA.
  9. 34MiyazakiThe wind valley. Roles pegged to a washing line; two panels where nothing happens.
  10. 35Es DevlinThe Revolve. Nine works on a turning drum; interests are empty seats.

All twelve use real content — Alfred_, the latency work, the five-verdict router, the three papers, Stellarium, SnAIder-Cut. The phrasing is a first pass at your voice and expects to be rewritten. Every spring carries its framer-motion config in a comment, so whichever you pick ports to React mechanically.
